Goose in a Pond

Connecting your calendar and mail

Your pond can read your calendar and the subject lines of your mail, so it knows what your week actually looks like. Everything below takes about five minutes per account.

First, the one thing that trips everyone up

You will not use your normal password. Every provider here wants an app-specific password — a separate password you generate for one app, which you can revoke on its own without changing anything else.

Why it matters. An app password only opens the one door you point it at. If you ever want the pond to stop reading your mail, you delete that one password and nothing else about your account changes.

Most providers only offer app passwords once two-factor authentication is switched on. If you get stuck at that step, that is usually why.

What the pond does with what it reads

TreatmentWhat
Kept Event title, time, place, who is invited
Kept Subject line, sender, and date
Never read The body of any message, and any attachment
Never done Sending, replying, deleting, or marking mail read

Mail bodies are left alone on purpose. A household's mail runs to hundreds of items a week, and keeping the whole of it would bury the things you would actually ask about.

Find your provider

Gmail

imap
  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification in your Google account, under Security.
  2. On the same Security page, open App passwords and create one. Name it something you will recognise, like "Pond".
  3. Copy the 16-character password Google shows you. It is only shown once.
  4. In Gmail, open Settings, then Forwarding and POP/IMAP, and switch IMAP on. This step is the one people miss.
  5. Connect the account as Gmail.

Google Calendar

not yet

Google Calendar needs you to sign in with Google, and this pond cannot do that yet. An app password will not work for it — Google refuses those for calendars specifically, which is why it is not offered rather than offered and broken.

Gmail above is unaffected. If you want a calendar today, an iCloud, Fastmail, or Nextcloud one works.

iCloud Calendar and Mail

caldav · imap
  1. Sign in to your Apple account and open Sign-In and Security.
  2. Choose App-Specific Passwords and generate one.
  3. Use your full iCloud email address as the username.
  4. Run the connect command with icloud — the same word works for both calendar and mail.

Apple offers no general-purpose API, so calendar over CalDAV and mail over IMAP is as far as any app can go. That is the ceiling, not a shortcut this pond is taking.

Fastmail Calendar and Mail

caldav · imap
  1. Open Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Connected apps.
  2. Create a new app password. Give it calendar access, mail access, or both.
  3. Run the connect command with fastmail.

Fastmail has the cleanest implementation of both protocols of any provider here. If you are choosing where to keep things, it is the one that gives the least trouble.

Nextcloud calendar

caldav
  1. Open Settings, then Security, and create a device password.
  2. Your server address is the one you use in a browser, for example https://cloud.example.org/remote.php/dav.
  3. Run the connect command with nextcloud and pass the server address as a fourth argument.

Any other mail server

imap
  1. Find your provider's IMAP server address — usually something like imap.example.org.
  2. Run the connect command with custom and pass the address as a fourth argument.
  3. Add :993 to the address only if your server uses a different port.

The pond connects over TLS on port 993 and will not fall back to an unencrypted connection.

Connect it

Easiest is the app: open Context, then Sources. If you would rather use a terminal, one command per account, run on the machine your pond is on.

bash scripts/connect-calendar.sh fastmail you@example.org YOUR-APP-PASSWORD

Swap fastmail for icloud or nextcloud. For Nextcloud or a custom server, add the address on the end:

bash scripts/connect-calendar.sh nextcloud you@example.org APP-PASSWORD https://cloud.example.org/remote.php/dav

The pond checks for new events and mail every half hour. The first check runs a minute or two after it starts, so you do not have to wait long to see whether it worked.

If something needs your attention

Each connected account shows one of three states.

  • Connected Working. The pond checked recently and found what it expected.
  • Needs attention The password was refused. Usually the app password was revoked, or an ordinary account password was used by mistake. The pond stops trying until you reconnect — it will not keep hammering your provider with a password that cannot work.
  • Paused Your pond is set to stay offline, so it is not reaching out to anything. Nothing is broken and nothing needs fixing.

Disconnecting an account deletes everything the pond read from it, and tells you how many items went. Your app password stays yours to revoke at your provider whenever you like.
